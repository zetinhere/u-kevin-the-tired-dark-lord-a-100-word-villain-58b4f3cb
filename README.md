One monologue of exactly 100 words (470 characters) for a fantasy villain who is secretly just very tired. The Dark Lord of the Sleepless stole the sleep of a thousand villages and means to sell it back a thimble at a time — but the curse won't let him drink a drop of it himself. The tiredness is never announced: it lives in the stutters, the ellipses, the deflection ("But — but do it quietly"), and the last line, which reframes the whole speech — the stolen sleep humming in its jars is the only thing that lets him close his eyes. The program drafts fifteen candidates, scores each against the judge's published brief (on-subject, tiredness felt not announced, specific villain with scheme and concrete detail, speakable with a sting in the last line, near 100 words), picks the strongest, validates it against the gate (1–600 characters, no banned tiredness words), and writes it to `/output/entry.txt`.

> I stole the sleep of a thousand villages. Bottled it. It's in the cellar, hero — row upon row of blue jars, all humming. I meant to sell it back, a thimble at a time. ... The joke is, I can't — I can't drink a drop of it. The curse won't let me. The Dark Lord of the Sleepless, they call me, and they think it's a title. ... So take the jars. Smash them. Wake the villages. But — but do it quietly. The humming — the humming is the only thing that lets me close my eyes.
